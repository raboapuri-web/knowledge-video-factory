# V96 Goosebumps — research grounding and visual safeguards

- Arrector pili muscles are smooth muscles associated with hair follicles and piloerection is mediated by sympathetic activity. Voluntary piloerection exists in rare individuals but should not be presented as typical.
- McPhetres et al. (2022), *The physiological study of emotional piloerection: A systematic review and guide for future research*, International Journal of Psychophysiology. PubMed PMID 35764195. The review synthesised 24 studies; emotional piloerection is often associated with phasic sympathetic indices such as skin conductance and heart rate, but the exact emotional mechanism remains unresolved.
- Heathers et al. (2018), *The voluntary control of piloerection*, PeerJ. PubMed PMID 30083447. Rare voluntary control is an exception, not normal human control.
- Salimpoor et al. (2011), *Anatomically distinct dopamine release during anticipation and experience of peak emotion to music*, Nature Neuroscience 14, 257–262. Dopamine release in striatum was observed during intense music-evoked pleasure; subjective chills and visible piloerection are related but not identical.
- Shwartz et al. (2020), *Cell Types Promoting Goosebumps Form a Niche to Regulate Hair Follicle Stem Cells*, Cell 182(3), 578–593.e19. PubMed PMID 32679029. The mechanistic stem-cell findings are primarily from mouse models; do not state that this proves why human goosebumps were evolutionarily retained.
- 2024 human study, *Diverse stimuli induce piloerection and yield varied autonomic responses in humans*, PMC11391818. Eight participants produced 1,198 observed piloerection events. This small study supports stimulus diversity and autonomic differences but not broad claims of strong thermoregulatory benefit in humans.
- Fever/chills section: fever raises the thermoregulatory set point, so shivering, vasoconstriction and piloerection can occur while the measured body temperature is already elevated.
- Darwin's historical discussion of hair/feather erection is used as historical context. Do not depict Darwin as proving a modern neural mechanism.

Visual policy: all participants, patients, historical observers and laboratory subjects are fictional illustrative composites. No real participant likenesses or real institutional logos. No readable book or paper facsimiles. Microscopic scenes are schematic educational visualizations, not literal histology. Animal behaviour is illustrative and non-violent. Music scenes show abstract orchestral performance and physiological response, not copyrighted music or score.
