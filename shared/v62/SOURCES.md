# V62 — Reference research and cautions

This documentary explores research associations, NOT a claim that low-income people as a class are more gullible, or that a specific social group is secretly controlling the economy.

- Adam-Troian et al. (2023), Economic precarity, institutional trust, and conspiracy beliefs, original publicly archived article: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10100481/
- Research on inequality perception, anomie, and conspiracy beliefs: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022103121001487
- Research review on three psychological motives (epistemic, existential, social) and conspiracy belief: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39913483/
- Douglas et al. (2017), The Psychology of Conspiracy Theories: https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721417718261
- U-shaped association in a US survey of socioeconomic position and conspiracy beliefs (2024): https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23780231241237654
- Relative deprivation is subjective and differs from annual income. Single-country cross-sectional associations cannot be generalized to all people or Japan, and no correlational study here demonstrates individual causal destiny.
- Alleged conspiracy and proven illegal collusion are not synonyms; show the need for credible independent evidence and willingness to update one's beliefs.
- Avoid real named public figures, partisan imagery, scapegoating groups, or unsupported claims that institutions, minorities or classes are secretly directing events. No moral/intellectual ranking of income groups.
- “Security, certainty, dignity” are possible motives, not diagnoses. The narrator's middle-aged worker and all accompanying people are fictional composite characters.
