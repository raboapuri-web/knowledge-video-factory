# V82 sources and historical-context checks

- The salaried worker, ticket prices ¥12,000 and ¥45,000, theatre broker, shop and resale scenes are hypothetical illustrations, not documented individuals or actual current sales. Onscreen visuals must not reproduce a real brand or confidential account.
- Culture Agency, Japan, Ticket Resale Law overview: https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunka_gyosei/ticket_resale_ban/ . Entered into force June 14, 2019; covers specified tickets and prescribed unauthorized business resale above face value, not every transfer or resale of every product.
- Phillip Leslie and Alan Sorensen, "Resale and Rent-Seeking: An Application to Ticket Markets," Review of Economic Studies 81(1), 266–300, 2014, DOI: 10.1093/restud/rdt033, https://academic.oup.com/restud/article-abstract/81/1/266/1730966 . Secondary-market allocation gains and costly primary-market rent-seeking are both relevant. Never imply experimental causal proof of universal benefit.
- 2009 NBER earlier working-paper version https://www.nber.org/papers/w15476 differs numerically from the published 2014 paper; do not conflate their quantitative estimates.
- Nobel Economics Prize 2007 laureates Hurwicz, Maskin and Myerson, mechanism design: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2007/summary/ .
- Postwar shortages, controlled prices and informal markets are a historical analogy only; do not equate survival food with entertainment goods or allege that all informal exchanges were predatory.
- The theory of supply, demand, arbitrage, incentives, opportunity cost and allocations are conceptual explanations, not empirical estimates for any specified Japanese ticket or gaming device.
- Different kinds of resale (new-goods arbitrage, face-value ticket return and used-goods exchange) must be clearly distinguished. No instructions to circumvent ticket rules or purchasing limits.
