V137 Poverty Premium — verified research & editorial scope (2026-10-25)

[1] University of Bristol, Fair By Design (June 2026).
"Paying More for Less: The Poverty Premium in 2026"
https://www.bristol.ac.uk/geography/research/pfrc/themes/financial-exclusion-poverty/poverty-premium-2026/
Low-income Britain, observed 95%, average £380/year. Not a Japanese estimate.

[2] Fair By Design, data methodology / survey.
https://fairbydesign.com/the-poverty-premium-in-2026/
Surveyed 1,040 respondents under 70% median household income, adjusted for size/housing costs.
Monthly car-insurance penalty amounts describe eligible affected cases, not all households.

[3] E. Mallick Hossain (2019/2020), "Less Is More Expensive: Income Differences in Bulk Buying"
https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3472425
Follow-up joint paper: Mallick Hossain & Christoph Bauner (2023),
"Less is More Expensive: Bulk Buying and Cognitive Costs"
https://people.umass.edu/cbauner/papers/BulkBuyingCogCosts.pdf
US grocery/Nielsen analysis; 5% is a modeled saving, not a universal causal effect.

[4] Mani, Mullainathan, Shafir & Zhao (2013), "Poverty Impedes Cognitive Function", Science 341, 976-980
https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1238041
Context-specific studies; cognitive-function claims require cautious interpretation.

[5] Giannella, Homonoff, Rino & Somerville (2024),
"Administrative Burden and Procedural Denials: Experimental Evidence from SNAP",
American Economic Journal: Economic Policy 16(4), 316-340
https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pol.20220701
Randomized field experiment: 65,000 LA applicants, approvals +6 percentage points.

[6] Haushofer & Shapiro (2016),
"The Short-term Impact of Unconditional Cash Transfers to the Poor:
Experimental Evidence from Kenya", Quarterly Journal of Economics 131(4)
https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjw025
Kenya RCT; lump-sum payments more likely spent on durables.
