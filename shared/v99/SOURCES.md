# V99 National Borders — original scene rebuild — research grounding and political-neutrality safeguards

This production explains border functions and historical development; it does not advocate stricter or looser border policy, immigration policy, territorial claims, or any party/candidate position. Distinguish the descriptive question "what functions do borders perform?" from the normative question "how restrictive should a particular border be?"

- Michael Biggs, "Putting the State on the Map: Cartography, Territory, and European State Formation," Comparative Studies in Society and History 41(2), 1999. Modern maps depict homogeneous territories bounded by lines; medieval political space often did not. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/comparative-studies-in-society-and-history/article/abs/putting-the-state-on-the-map-cartography-territory-and-european-state-formation/72EAFF604C154FE2D74D6E8446849704
- Jordan Branch, "Mapping the Sovereign State: Technology, Authority, and Systemic Change," International Organization 65(1), 2011. Cartographic technologies helped shape linear boundaries and homogeneous territorial authority; do not narrate maps as the only cause of state formation. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/mapping-the-sovereign-state-technology-authority-and-systemic-change/0C0A5F17B945CFE437DA681D2C5FE025
- Avoid the simplistic claim that modern sovereignty was created overnight at Westphalia in 1648. Branch notes the Münster treaty used lists of places/rights rather than cartographic boundary language; territorial state formation was gradual.
- United Nations Charter Article 2: sovereign equality and prohibition on threat/use of force against territorial integrity or political independence. https://legal.un.org/repertory/art2.shtml
- John Helliwell, NBER Working Paper 5215 (1995), extending McCallum's 1988 Canada-US trade border effect. The original headline effect was extremely large; later Anderson & van Wincoop work substantially reduced estimates. Do not present 22x as a timeless causal constant. https://www.nber.org/papers/w5215 and https://www.nber.org/papers/w8079
- Anam Soomro, "People, Paper and Power: The Birth of the Passport in International Law," Asian Journal of International Law 14(2), 2024. Modern passport/visa mobility governance expanded in the early twentieth century; League of Nations conferences initially discussed abolition before standardization prevailed. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/asian-journal-of-international-law/article/people-paper-and-power-the-birth-of-the-passport-in-international-law/9D47478763A0E03C1EAE1013DE649B84
- European Commission / Council of the EU: as of 2026, Schengen has 29 full members and enables more than 450 million people to travel without routine internal border controls; Bulgaria and Romania became full members on 1 January 2025. Internal free movement is paired with common external-border and law-enforcement cooperation. https://commission.europa.eu/topics/borders_en and https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/schengen-area/
- Michalopoulos & Papaioannou, NBER Working Paper 17620, "The Long-Run Effects of the Scramble for Africa." Partitioned historical ethnic homelands are associated with higher violence and lower public-goods access in their data. This is an empirical historical association and research design, not a claim that all African borders have identical effects. https://www.nber.org/papers/w17620
