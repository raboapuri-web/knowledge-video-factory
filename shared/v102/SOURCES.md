# V102 — Research grounding and scene truthfulness

Title: 「弱者を救う制度」が、別の弱者を生む【社会保障論×行動経済学×制度設計×貧困の社会学】

Visual persons in the two Japanese apartments, the English workhouse family, the US mother, and the French tenants are hypothetical explanatory characters. Do not describe their private particulars as actual case records or reconstruct any identifiable study participants. Historical and empirical facts below are separate from these illustrative scenes.

- Fukai, Taiyo and Kondo, Ayako (2024), Economic and Social Research Institute Discussion Paper No. 387, Cabinet Office of Japan: "認可保育所入所の可否が両親の就労・所得に与える影響". Administrative records, near-identical enrollment probabilities: accredited nursery access raised employment of mothers of 0- and 1-year-olds by 40.3 and 18.8 percentage points, respectively. Population and year are specific; do not generalize these point estimates nationally. https://www.esri.cao.go.jp/jp/esri/archive/e_dis/2024/e_dis387.html
- Hansard, UK Commons, 14 May 1834, Poor Laws Amendment Committee: debates family separation and hardship to involuntarily unemployed workers. The 1834 law and indoor/outdoor relief had local variation; do not suggest every poor person was automatically confined. https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1834/may/14/poor-laws-amendment-committee
- Hansard, UK Commons, 26 May 1834, Poor Laws Amendment Committee: arguments concerning the workhouse test and limits of in/outdoor relief. https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1834/may/26/poor-laws-amendment-committee
- Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta, Career Ladder Identifier and Financial Forecaster (CLIFF). Benefit cliffs depend on household, geography, benefit combination, taxes and income; the $200 wage example is illustrative, not an estimated effect. https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/data/cliff-tool
- Gabrielle Fack (2006), "Are housing benefit an effective way to redistribute income? Evidence from a natural experiment in France", Labour Economics 13(6), 747-771. For new benefit claimants in this reform, a one-euro increase in housing benefit was associated with 78-cent higher rents on average; substantial rent capture reflected low housing-supply elasticity in that setting. Not a universal subsidy-incidence coefficient. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.labeco.2006.01.001
- Hoynes and Schanzenbach (2018), "Safety Net Investments in Children", NBER Working Paper 24594, US child safety-net growth since 1990 largely accrued to families with earnings and families above the poverty line. Distinguish earned-income supports from separate disability and nonworking household supports; do not claim the former abolishes the latter. https://www.nber.org/papers/w24594
- KFF, Tolbert and Corallo (18 September 2024), "An Examination of Medicaid Renewal Outcomes and Enrollment Changes at the End of the Unwinding". More than 25m disenrolled, 69% of disenrollments for procedural or paperwork reasons. Procedural disenrollment does not prove that every affected person retained eligibility. https://www.kff.org/medicaid/an-examination-of-medicaid-renewal-outcomes-and-enrollment-changes-at-the-end-of-the-unwinding/
- Hoynes, Schanzenbach and Almond (2016), "Long-Run Impacts of Childhood Access to the Safety Net", American Economic Review 106(4), 903-934. Early-life food stamp access reduced later metabolic syndrome; for women, increased economic self-sufficiency in the study. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20130375
