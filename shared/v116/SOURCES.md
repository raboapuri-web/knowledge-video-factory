# V116 Sources

## Hunter S. Thompson / Gonzo framing
- Thompson's 1970 article “The Kentucky Derby Is Decadent and Depraved,” produced with illustrator Ralph Steadman, is widely described as the birth or an originating work of Gonzo journalism.
- The production uses Thompson as a narrative example of a public persona becoming culturally expected. It does **not** claim that his whole personality was fabricated or provide a clinical diagnosis.

## Narrative identity
- McAdams, D. P., & McLean, K. C. (2013). “Narrative Identity.” *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, 22(3), 233–238. DOI: 10.1177/0963721413475622.
- Core point used: narrative identity is an internalized and evolving life story integrating reconstructed past and imagined future.

## Identity theory / identity verification
- Burke, P. J., & Stets, J. E. (2022/2023). *Identity Theory: Revised and Expanded*, 2nd ed. Oxford University Press.
- Core points used: identity standards, perceived self-in-situation meanings, discrepancy/verification, multiple identities and identity change.

## Self-presentation carry-over
- Schlenker, B. R., Dlugolecki, D. W., & Doherty, K. (1994). “The Impact of Self-Presentations on Self-Appraisals and Behavior: The Power of Public Commitment.” *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 20(1). DOI: 10.1177/0146167294201002.
- Core point used: presenting oneself as sociable influenced later self-appraisals and behavior in a different setting.

## Possible selves
- Markus, H., & Nurius, P. (1986). “Possible Selves.” *American Psychologist*, 41(9), 954–969.
- Core point used: people represent what they might become, would like to become and fear becoming; these possible selves can motivate present behavior.

## Flexible vs fixed representations of self
- Rabinovich, A., & Morton, T. A. (2016). “Coping with Identity Conflict: Perceptions of Self as Flexible versus Fixed Moderate the Effect of Identity Conflict on Well-being.” *Self and Identity*, 15(2), 224–244. DOI: 10.1080/15298868.2015.1117524.
- Four experimental studies found that conflicting identities reduced well-being/self-esteem among participants construing self as stable, but not among those adopting flexible self-representations.

## Framing note
“人格の後方互換性” is an original explanatory metaphor for this video, **not** an established academic term. It describes a situation in which relationships, income, reputation or expectations become dependent on a previously established self-image, raising the cost of identity change.
