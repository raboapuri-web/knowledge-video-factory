# V107 research and source notes

This film is an original script and original 2D production inspired only by the broad theme of the user-supplied video URL. No third-party narration, footage, score or illustrations are reused.

## Psychology / behavioral science
1. Michael Inzlicht, Amitai Shenhav, Christopher Y. Olivola (2018), “The Effort Paradox: Effort Is Both Costly and Valued”, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 22(4), 337–349. DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2018.01.007. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6172040/
   - Review: effort is generally costly/avoided, while effort can also add value to outcomes and sometimes to effort itself.
2. Michael I. Norton, Daniel Mochon, Dan Ariely (2012), “The IKEA Effect: When Labor Leads to Love”, Journal of Consumer Psychology 22(3), 453–460. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.002
   - Labor can increase valuation of self-created products; successful completion matters.
3. Daniel Mochon, Michael I. Norton, Dan Ariely (2012), “Bolstering and restoring feelings of competence via the IKEA effect”, International Journal of Research in Marketing 29(4), 363–369. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijresmar.2012.05.001.
4. Robert Eisenberger (1992), “Learned industriousness”, Psychological Review 99(2), 248–267. DOI: 10.1037/0033-295X.99.2.248. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1594725/
   - Review/theory on rewarded effort and learned willingness to exert effort.
5. Angelos-Miltiadis Krypotos, Marieke Effting, Merel Kindt, Tom Beckers (2015), “Avoidance learning: a review of theoretical models and recent developments”, Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience 9:189. DOI: 10.3389/fnbeh.2015.00189. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4508580/
   - Avoidance can prevent corrective information and become maladaptive; genuine threat avoidance remains adaptive.
6. Mark D. Seery, E. Alison Holman, Roxane Cohen Silver (2010), “Whatever does not kill us: cumulative lifetime adversity, vulnerability, and resilience”, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 99(6), 1025–1041. DOI: 10.1037/a0021344. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20939649/
   - Some nonzero prior adversity was associated with better later outcomes than either high adversity or no adversity in that sample. This does not justify intentionally inflicting adversity.
7. Philip Brickman, Dan Coates, Ronnie Janoff-Bulman (1978), “Lottery winners and accident victims: Is happiness relative?”, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 36(8), 917–927. DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.36.8.917. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/690806/
   - Classic adaptation-level study; sample included 22 major lottery winners, 22 controls and 29 accident victims from a prior comparison sample.

## Ancient philosophy
8. Seneca, Moral Letters to Lucilius, Letter 18. https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_18
   - Seneca recommends occasional days with cheap food, coarse dress and a simple pallet to test feared deprivation.
9. Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus. https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/menoec.html
   - Epicurus explicitly argues that not every pleasure should be chosen and not every pain avoided; simple fare can reduce dependence on luxury.

Interpret philosophical material historically. The production does not present ancient practices as medical or psychological treatment.
