# V109 Sources — なぜ無能の社員ほど、自分がエースのように振る舞うのか？

The film is based on the accepted narration and paraphrases the following research anchors rather than reproducing source text.

- Kruger, J. & Dunning, D. (1999). Unskilled and unaware of it: How difficulties in recognizing one's own incompetence lead to inflated self-assessments. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 77(6), 1121–1134. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10626367/
- Gignac, G. E. & Zajenkowski, M. (2022). Statistical / methodological discussion of the Dunning–Kruger effect and regression-to-the-mean style explanations. Frontiers in Psychology: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.840180/full
- Han, Y. & Dunning, D. (2024). Expert and non-expert metacognition across climate science, psychological statistics, and investing. Behavioral Decision Making: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bdm.2375
- Anderson, C., Brion, S., Moore, D. A., & Kennedy, J. A. (2012). A status-enhancement account of overconfidence. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22800286/
- Large-scale meta-analytic evidence on confidence–accuracy relations, referenced in the accepted narration: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10777204/

Production note: the film does not treat “low ability = always overconfident” as a universal law. It explicitly distinguishes self-assessment error, statistical artifacts, metacognition, expertise, confidence displays, and organizational status dynamics.
