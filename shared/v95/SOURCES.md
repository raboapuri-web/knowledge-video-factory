# V95 Basic Income — research grounding and production caveats
- World Bank, *Exploring Universal Basic Income* (2020): UBI definitions, design choices, fiscal and macro limits. Pilot evidence does not reproduce all nationwide financing and price effects.
- Speenhamland (1795) is an early income-support scheme, not modern universal basic income.
- Thomas Spence / Thomas Paine: historical proposals concerning common claims on land or natural wealth; do not present either as a modern implemented UBI.
- U.S. Negative Income Tax experiments, late 1960s–1970s: distinguish means-tested income guarantees from universal unconditional payments.
- Evelyn Forget, Dauphin MINCOME re-analysis: hospitalization and schooling findings concern a specific Canadian quasi-experimental context.
- Jones & Marinescu, Alaska Permanent Fund dividend: broad annual dividend, not a full living-income UBI; employment and part-time findings are state-specific.
- Finland basic income experiment 2017–2018: sample was 2,000 unemployed people; employment effects were modest and second-year interpretation is complicated by other policy changes. Survey well-being outcomes have response limitations.
- OpenResearch unconditional cash study, 2020–2023: low-income U.S. adults, monthly cash; labor supply reductions were modest, spending rose, and not all health/wealth outcomes improved.
- GiveDirectly Kenya long-term basic income study: monthly long/short duration and lump-sum treatments differ. Rural Kenyan results do not mechanically generalize to Japan or national tax-financed UBI.
- Egger et al. Kenya cash-transfer general-equilibrium study: local spillovers and limited average price effects in that setting; not proof that a national UBI cannot generate inflation.
- OECD basic-income simulations: replacing existing benefits can create losers among people with high needs; gross benefit cost and net fiscal cost are different.
- AI/automation section is a scenario discussion, not a prediction that mass technological unemployment is inevitable.
