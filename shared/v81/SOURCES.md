# V81 — scholarly grounding and narration safeguards

## Historical terms
Richard von Krafft-Ebing, Psychopathia Sexualis (1886), coined the term masochism drawing on Leopold von Sacher-Masoch and his novel Venus in Furs (1870). The term's historical creation must never be equated to the first occurrence of the underlying interests.

## Empirical research
Joyal, C. C. & Carpentier, J. (2017), The Prevalence of Paraphilic Interests and Behaviors in the General Population: A Provincial Survey, Journal of Sex Research. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26941021/ . The approximately-half finding concerns at least one of multiple non-normative interests in a Quebec sample, not masochism prevalence.
Holvoet, L. et al. (2017), Fifty Shades of Belgian Gray: The Prevalence of BDSM-Related Fantasies and Activities in the General Population. Interest is not identical to actual behavior; BDSM is broader than masochism.
Wuyts, E. & Morrens, M. (2019), The Biology of BDSM: A Systematic Review. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31617765/ . The review discusses several mechanisms and limitations; mental illness and prior trauma must not be posited as a universal explanation.
2016 neuroimaging study of pain perception in sexual masochism, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26808014/ . Small sample, specific visual/stimulus context; do not assert universal or zero pain.
2020 research into motivations of BDSM submission and sexual masochism, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32486920/ . Distinct motivations should not be collapsed to mere pleasure in injury.
2024 evolutionary account examining BDSM as potentially emerging from broader mechanisms, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38769280/ . The evolutionary account is hypothetical, not proof of a specific gene, adaptation, reproductive benefit, or ancestral prevalence.
American Psychiatric Association, DSM-5 paraphilic disorders: distinguish consensual atypical interests from disorders meeting clinical diagnostic criteria. https://www.psychiatry.org/File%20Library/Psychiatrists/Practice/DSM/APA_DSM-5-Paraphilic-Disorders.pdf

## Ethical and platform-safe visual principles
No sexual act, fetish items, nudity, exposed skin close-ups, suggestive touch, injuries, torture, coercion or sensual imagery. Historical primary sources presented as anonymous paper pages, books with non-legible strokes, archival room and clean scientist diagrams. Abstract circles/keys/arrows illustrate agency and context. Two people are shown only at conversational distance, fully clothed, in well-lit everyday settings. Narration describes contested science with qualifiers and keeps consent central. Illustrative characters are fictional, not the actual historical figures or study participants. Do not portray ancient manuscripts as proof of universal, unchanged modern categories. Text overlays only spoken Japanese subtitles.
