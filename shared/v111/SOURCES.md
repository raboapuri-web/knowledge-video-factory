# V111 Sources — なぜ生活保護がなくなると社会は崩壊するのか？

The production preserves the accepted narration. Research anchors used by the script:

- 厚生労働省「生活保護制度」: https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/hukushi_kaigo/seikatsuhogo/seikatuhogo/index.html
- 日本「生活保護法」第1条（最低生活保障・自立助長）。
- Sadowski et al. Housing and case management for homeless adults with chronic medical illnesses: randomized trial, JAMA (2009): https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/183842
- Stergiopoulos et al./At Home-Chez Soi cost and service-use analyses / JAMA Network Open housing-first evidence: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2748596
- NBER review on economic conditions / crime: https://www.nber.org/papers/w32297
- Deshpande & Mueller-Smith, SSI removal and criminal justice involvement: https://www.nber.org/papers/w29800
- Danish welfare-benefit cuts and crime spillovers: https://www.nber.org/papers/w33926
- Finnish basic-income experiment and crime outcomes: https://www.nber.org/papers/w34547
- OECD automatic fiscal stabilisers: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/how-effective-are-automatic-fiscal-stabilisers-in-the-oecd-countries_f1fb9d6a-en.html
- OECD social safety nets and structural adjustment / incentives: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/social-safety-nets-and-structural-adjustment_418374332425.html

Production framing:
- The film does not portray low-income people as inherently criminal.
- “Crime” sequences visualize constrained options and public-cost shifts, not moral character.
- International studies are visually labeled as external evidence and not presented as direct estimates of Japanese livelihood protection.
- The thesis is not that any welfare level is optimal; it is that removing the social floor can shift or magnify costs elsewhere.
