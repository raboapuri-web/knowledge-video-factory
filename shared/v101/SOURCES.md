# V101 Greek mythology — primary and scholarly reference notes

The video introduces Greek mythic tradition to a first-time viewer without retelling the plot of a particular modern film. Mythic episodes are presented as traditions, not verified historical events. Variant traditions are acknowledged.

- Hesiod, *Theogony*: Chaos, Gaia, Ouranos, Kronos, Zeus, divine succession, the Cyclopes and Titanomachy. Perseus Digital Library: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/
- Hesiod, *Works and Days*, early verses: Prometheus, Pandora and the jar, the term *elpis*. Perseus Digital Library: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/
- Homer, *Iliad* (esp. divine interventions and Achilles) and *Odyssey* (Athena, Poseidon, xenia, nostos, Odysseus). Texts at Perseus: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/
- Homeric Hymn to Demeter: Demeter and Persephone. Theophany and ritual context in the Homeric Hymns. Perseus: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/
- Apollodorus, *Library*: mythographic variants for Perseus, Theseus, Heracles and the Trojan cycle. Perseus: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/
- Walter Burkert, *Greek Religion*, Harvard University Press (1985): ritual and the polytheistic civic context.
- Robert Parker, *On Greek Religion*, Cornell University Press (2011): cult, sacrifice and the relationship between myth and religious practice.
- M. L. West, *Hesiod: Theogony*, Oxford University Press: commentary on divine generations.
- Jasper Griffin, *Homer on Life and Death*, Oxford University Press: heroic values and Homeric mortality.

Cautions: *Theogony* is one influential genealogy, not the only version. In Hesiod Pandora opens a *pithos* (jar), not originally a box. Interpretations of *elpis* differ. The Trojan horse episode is part of the wider Trojan cycle and is not told in full in the *Iliad*. Modern film-specific details are not asserted or anticipated.
