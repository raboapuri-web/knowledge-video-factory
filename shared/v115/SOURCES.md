# V115 Sources

## Performance appraisal and cognitive bias
- Bellé, N., Cantarelli, P., & Belardinelli, P. (2017). “Cognitive Biases in Performance Appraisal: Experimental Evidence on Anchoring and Halo Effects With Public Sector Managers and Employees.” *Public Personnel Management*, 46? / SAGE article DOI: 10.1177/0734371X17704891. The study reports two artefactual field experiments with 600 public-sector managers and employees and finds anchoring and halo effects biased performance ratings.
- Balzer, W. K., & Sulsky, L. M. (1992). “Halo and performance appraisal research: A critical examination.” *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 77, 975–985.

## Leader–Member Exchange
- Graen, G. B., & Uhl-Bien, M. (1995). “Relationship-Based Approach to Leadership: Development of Leader-Member Exchange (LMX) Theory of Leadership over 25 Years.” *The Leadership Quarterly*, 6. DOI: 10.1016/1048-9843(95)90036-5.
- Gerstner, C. R., & Day, D. V. (1997). “Meta-analytic review of leader–member exchange theory: Correlates and construct issues.” *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 82, 827–844.

## Political skill, social resources, career outcomes
- Ferris, G. R., et al. (2005). “Development and validation of the Political Skill Inventory.” *Journal of Management*, 31(1), 126–152. DOI: 10.1177/0149206304271386.
- Ferris, G. R., et al. (2007). “Political skill in organizations.” *Journal of Management*, 33(3), 290–320. DOI: 10.1177/0149206307300813.
- Todd, S. Y., Harris, K. J., Harris, R. B., & Wheeler, A. R. (2009). “Career success implications of political skill.” *The Journal of Social Psychology*, 149(3), 279–304. DOI: 10.3200/SOCP.149.3.279-304.
- Sibunruang, H., Garcia, P. R. J. M., & Tolentino, L. - Sibunruang, H., Garcia, P. R. J. M., & Tolentino, L. R. (2016). “Ingratiation as an adapting strategy: Its relationship with career adaptability, career sponsorship, and promotability.” *Journal of Vocational Behavior*, 92, 135–144. DOI: 10.1016/j.jvb.2015.11.011.

## Framing note
This video does **not** claim that likable employees are less competent, that political skill always causes promotion, or that measurable performance is irrelevant. The production frames promotion as a judgment under incomplete information, where performance, visibility, relationship quality, social resources and future-role expectations can interact.
